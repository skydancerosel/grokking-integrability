\section*{1.\quad Problem}
\vspace{-0.4em}

\citet{tian2025li2} proves a repulsion theorem (Theorem~6) for two-layer
networks $\widehat Y = \sigma(XW)V$ ($\sigma(x){=}x^2$, MSE loss with sample-mean
projector $P_1^\perp$) during the interactive feature-learning stage of
grokking. The asserted off-diagonal sign rule for $B := (\Ftil^\top\Ftil + \eta I)^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
\sgn(B_{j\ell}) \;=\; -\sgn\!\left(\widetilde f_j^\top P_{\eta,-j\ell}
\widetilde f_\ell\right),
\qquad
P_{\eta,-j\ell} := I - \Ftil_{-j\ell}(\Ftil_{-j\ell}^\top\Ftil_{-j\ell} +
\eta I)^{-1}\Ftil_{-j\ell}^\top.
\label{eq:thm6}
\end{equation}
Two questions the framework does not answer empirically: (i)~when does
this become observable in training, and (ii)~does it generalize beyond
the $\sigma(x){=}x^2$ regime in which it is proven?

% ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{2.\quad Result}
\vspace{-0.4em}

\textbf{The mechanism is activation-general; its spectral signature is
activation-specific.}

\paragraph{Theorem 6 verification across activations.} Computing $B$ via
the Woodbury identity,
\begin{equation}
B = \tfrac{1}{\eta} I - \tfrac{1}{\eta^2}\Ftil^\top
(\Ftil \Ftil^\top + \eta I)^{-1}\Ftil
\quad\text{($K\times K$ inverse $\to$ $n\times n$, float64 on CPU)},
\label{eq:woodbury}
\end{equation}
on top-200 most-similar feature pairs at five deterministic-replay
checkpoints, multi-seed sign-match progression on Tian's exact setup
($M=71$, $K=2048$, $n=2016$, $\eta=2\!\times\!10^{-4}$):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{rcccccc}
\toprule
epoch & 50 & 100 & \textbf{175 (lock-in)} & 250 & 300 & range \\
\midrule
$\sigma=x^2$ ($n=5$ seeds) & 0.865 & 0.895 & \textbf{0.955} & 0.970 & \textbf{0.985} & IQR $\le 0.015$ \\
$\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}$ (seed 0) & --- & 0.91 & --- & --- & 0.995 (ep 300), 1.000 (ep 500) & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $P_{\eta,-j\ell} \approx P_\eta$ approximation preserves the sign
rule on directly-checked pairs (10/10 sign agreement at epoch~175
seed~0); the magnitude of the residual differs because the full
projector $P_\eta$ nearly annihilates $\widetilde f_\ell$ while
$P_{\eta,-j\ell}$ does not, but for the sign rule of
equation~\eqref{eq:thm6} the approximation is sufficient.

\paragraph{Parameter-update spectral signature.} At each epoch we form
the rolling-window Gram of $\Delta W$ ($W=20$, $P=2MK$):
\begin{equation}
\Delta := [\Delta W_{t-W+1}, \ldots, \Delta W_t] \in \R^{P \times W},
\quad
\sigma_k(t) := \lambda_k(\Delta^\top \Delta).
\label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
A slope-based detector,
\begin{equation}
s(t) := \tfrac{1}{25}\bigl[\log(\sigma_2/\sigma_3)(t) -
\log(\sigma_2/\sigma_3)(t-25)\bigr],
\quad \text{``fire''} := \min\{t \ge 100 : s(t) > 0.04\},
\label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
gives, on $\sigma=x^2$ at the headline operating point ($\eta=2\!\times\!10^{-4}$
vs $\eta=0$, 15 seeds each):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{15/15 grok seeds fire at epoch 174 (IQR $[173, 174]$);
0/15 control seeds.} Late-stage magnitude separation $229\times$
($\sigma_2/\sigma_3 \approx 300$ grok vs $\approx 1.31$ control over
epochs 200--400). The slope-fire epoch coincides with Theorem 6
saturation ($\ge 0.95$).
\item Mechanism: between epochs 150 and 200, $\sigma_2$ stabilizes near
$5\!\times\!10^{-3}$ while $\sigma_3$ collapses two orders of magnitude.
The rolling spectrum becomes effectively rank-2.
\end{itemize}

On $\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}$ (15 seeds, 800 epochs, otherwise identical):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{0/15 grok seeds fire.} Late-stage magnitude separation
collapses to $1.4\times$. The spectrum is rank-1 dominated:
$\sigma_1 \gg \sigma_2 \approx \sigma_3 \approx \sigma_4 \approx \sigma_5$.
\end{itemize}

% ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{3.\quad Interpretation: structure vs mechanism}
\vspace{-0.4em}

Tian's Theorem 6 governs the off-diagonal sign structure of $B$ via
properties of $\Ftil^\top\Ftil$ alone, which depends on the activation
only through its effect on $\Ftil$. The rolling-$\Delta W$ spectral
signature of Theorem 6's saturation depends on \emph{how} the repulsion
translates into weight updates, which depends on $\sigma'$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item Power activations ($\sigma(x){=}x^2$) produce \emph{focused
memorization} (\citealp{tian2025li2} Theorem 5): features collapse onto
sharp peaks. Theorem 6 repulsion consolidates redundant features onto
two persistent rank-2 directions in $\Delta W$; we observe this directly.
\item ReLU/sigmoid activations produce \emph{spreading memorization}:
features remain distributed. Theorem 6 still operates (sign rule still
holds), but there are no sharp peaks for repulsion to consolidate around;
$\Delta W$ remains rank-1 dominated.
\end{itemize}

The two findings together identify which parts of the \texttt{Li}$_2$
framework are activation-general (Theorem 6's sign rule itself) versus
activation-specific (the rolling-$\Delta W$ rank-2 signature).

\section*{4.\quad Methodological controls}
\vspace{-0.4em}

\paragraph{Window-size sensitivity ($n=3$ seeds, $\sigma=x^2$):}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{rcccc}
\toprule
$W$ & grok fire (3 seeds) & ctrl fire (3 seeds) & late $\sigma_2/\sigma_3$ grok & late $\sigma_2/\sigma_3$ ctrl \\
\midrule
5  & [144, 149, 153] & \textbf{[179, 193, 263] (3/3 fire)} & 408 & 8.0 \\
10 & [---, ---, 347] (1/3) & \textbf{[263, 266, 278] (3/3)} & 64  & 2.3 \\
\textbf{20} & [173, 173, 174] & [---, ---, ---] (0/3) & 285 & 1.3 \\
30 & [180, 180, 180] & [---, ---, ---] (0/3) & 140 & 1.2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$W \in \{20, 30\}$ give perfect specificity; $W \le 10$ produce false
positives in control. The transition is $\sim 25$ epochs wide, so the
window must average over this without smearing into Stage~I/II.

\paragraph{Rank confirmation via $\sigma_4, \sigma_5$:} At $W \le 10$,
$\sigma_3, \sigma_4, \sigma_5$ collapse together to $\sim 10^{-5}$
(exact rank-2). At $W=30$, geometric cascade $\sigma_3 \approx 10^{-4}$,
$\sigma_4 \approx 10^{-5}$, $\sigma_5 \approx 10^{-6}$ (rank $\lesssim 3$).
The "rank-2" framing is exact at the finest temporal resolution.

% ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{5.\quad Extension across $\eta$ and the level-metric initiation detector}
\vspace{-0.4em}

\paragraph{$\eta$ sweep ($n=25$ runs, $\sigma=x^2$).} An extended single-seed
run at $\eta=10^{-5}$ to 2000 epochs confirms Tian's $1/\eta$ scaling:
grokking at epoch~1527, with the level metric
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{tian}}(t) := \frac{\norm{P_1^\perp F F^\top -
(a(t) I + b(t)\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top)}_F}{\norm{F F^\top}_F}
\label{eq:rho-tian}
\end{equation}
crossing 0.075 at epoch~527 (lead 567 epochs vs $t_{\text{test}=0.5}$).
At larger $\eta$ the lead is positive in 20/20 grokking runs (range
79--154 epochs).

The lock-in magnitude is $\eta$-dependent: at $\eta=10^{-5}$ peak
$\sigma_2/\sigma_3 \approx 25$ vs $\approx 300$ at $\eta=2\!\times\!10^{-4}$.
The rank-2 structure is incompletely developed when measurement ends in
the slow regime, but its existence is preserved.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{eta_sweep_scatter.png}
\caption{$\eta$ sweep ($n=25$, $\sigma=x^2$): $\rho_{\mathrm{tian}}$
fire epoch versus $t_{\text{test}=0.5}$. Lead positive in 20/20
grokking runs; lead time scales with grokking timescale. Linear
regression $t_{\rho} = 0.80\, t_{\text{test}} - 75$ ($R^2 = 0.87$).}
\label{fig:eta-sweep}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Tightly-scoped applicability.} $\rho_{\mathrm{tian}}$ is a
Stage~I$\to$II initiation marker but does NOT survive an $M \times p$
scaling sweep: on a 60-run sweep ($M \in \{41, 71, 127\}$, $p \in
\{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$, 5 seeds), it fires in \textbf{60/60 runs}
regardless of grokking outcome. Nor does it survive activation change:
on $\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}$ it fires at epoch~0 because the ReLU
initialization is already far from the form $aI + b\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$
(asymmetric activation $\Rightarrow$ non-isotropic random-feature Gram).
The detector is scoped to activations producing approximately isotropic
random features at init.
